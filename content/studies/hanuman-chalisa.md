## Introduction

The Hanuman Chalisa is a 40-verse Hindu devotional hymn composed by Saint Tulsidas in the Awadhi language to praise Lord Hanuman.  It is a powerful prayer dedicated to Hanuman's strength, courage, wisdom, and devotion to Lord Rama, commonly recited for protection, strength, and overcoming obstacles.  

Hanuman is a Hindu deity and a devotee of the Hindu god, Rama. He is one of the central characters of the Ramayana. According to the Shaiva tradition, he is also an incarnation of Shiva. The Hanuman Chalisa praises the power and other qualities of Hanuman including his strength, courage, wisdom, celibacy (brahmacharya), and devotion to Rama. [ref](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman_Chalisa)

## Related

- The physical discipline described in this hymn — strength, courage, control — also informs the martial lineage covered in [[../kalari/index|Kalaripayattu]].

## Legend

According to popular historical legend, the timing of its creation is tied to a specific event in Tulsidas's life involving the Mughal Emperor Akbar.  Emperor Akbar supposedly heard of Tulsidas's devotion and miracles. When summoned to the court at Fatehpur Sikri and asked to perform a miracle, Tulsidas refused, stating he was merely a devotee of Lord Rama. Angered by this response, Akbar imprisoned him. It is believed that during his confinement, Tulsidas wrote the Hanuman Chalisa over the course of 40 days, composing one verse (chaupai) each day. Legend has it that upon the completion of the 40th verse, an army of monkeys swarmed the prison and the city, forcing Akbar to realize his mistake and release the saint.  
The word "Chalisa" is derived from "Chalis," which means forty in Hindi, reflecting both the 40 verses of the prayer and the 40 days he spent composing it in captivity
